A paper is more than its .tex files. meertex keeps all of it.

meertex is a LaTeX editor for the whole life of a paper, from the call for papers to camera-ready. Each paper carries its venue, its deadlines with countdowns and every version you handed in. meertex checks that your citations exist, and reviewers see how each of their comments was addressed.

You write on your own computer, in VS Code, vim or together with an LLM agent. Every save reaches your coauthors within a second. They edit the same document in the browser, with live cursors and a PDF that compiles as they type. A command line tool and an API make all of it ready for your LLM workflow.

Start writing Watch the tour View on GitHub

Free to use at meertex.com. Sign in with your email address, no password needed. meertex is open source, and the README explains how to run it on your own server. It is part of the Meerverse, the family of meer* apps.

How it works

What GitHub is for code, meertex is for papers

GitHub keeps a project's code in one place, with its history, its releases and the reviews around it. meertex does the same for a paper and adds what only papers have: a venue, deadlines and a bibliography that has to hold up. There is nothing to commit or push. Every save reaches all your computers and every coauthor within a second.

  • Repository

    Paper

    Its files and coauthors together with its venue, every deadline and the outcome.

  • Push and pull

    Live sync

    A normal folder on each of your computers that follows every edit, from you and from everyone else.

  • Commit history

    History

    The server commits the paper to git while you write, so every earlier state is still there.

  • Pull request review

    Reviews

    Comments on the text or right on the PDF, suggestions applied with one click and an email to everyone involved.

  • Releases and tags

    Versions

    Submission, rebuttal, revision and camera-ready, kept for good with their PDFs. Any two compare as a PDF with every change marked.

  • CI checks

    Checks

    A PDF two seconds after the last edit, spelling and grammar as you type, and every citation looked up at Crossref, DBLP and arXiv.

  • Milestones

    Deadlines

    Every date in the venue's time zone, with countdowns, reminder emails and a question once it has passed.

  • Forks

    Resubmissions

    A rejected paper goes to the next venue as a fork that keeps its files and links back.

Like git, without commit and push

Features

Everything a paper needs

Next to its text, a paper has coauthors, a venue, deadlines, the versions you handed in, reviews and a bibliography that has to hold up. meertex looks after each of them.

Your folder, always up to date

meertex keeps the paper's folder on your computer in step with everyone else's edits. Files that a coauthor deleted go to a trash folder, so nothing is lost. The files LaTeX writes while it compiles, such as .aux files and your local PDF, stay on your computer. 

$ meertex checkout 2027-icse-green-llm
$ meertex service install

Coauthors in the browser

The browser editor works on the same document as your checkout, with everyone's cursor and name. Autocomplete knows LaTeX commands and environments, every \label in the project, the keys of every .bib file and the paths for \input and \includegraphics. Invite people by email as editors, reviewers or viewers.

A PDF that keeps up

The PDF compiles two seconds after the last edit, with pdfLaTeX, XeLaTeX or LuaLaTeX. Errors and warnings show up in a list, and a click opens the right line. Click the PDF to jump to the source, or jump from the text to its spot in the PDF.

Every paper knows its venue

Conference, track, links, page limit, template and every date from abstract registration to camera-ready, in the venue's time zone (AoE by default). Countdowns turn amber two weeks out and red in the last three days. A deadline that moved says so.

Paper submissionin 2 days Notification, moved from 31 Octin 9 days Camera-readyin 7 weeks

It asks when a date passes

Once a deadline is over, meertex asks what happened. “Did you hand it in?” freezes the submission version from the state at the deadline, even when you answer three days late. “Was it accepted?” sets the outcome, so all your accepted papers are one filter away.

Did you hand it in? Did you send a rebuttal? Was the paper accepted?

Versions you can point to

Submission, rebuttal, Revision N and camera-ready are saved for good, with the compiled PDF, the source as a zip and the PDF you actually uploaded. Compare any two by lines, by words, or as a PDF with every change marked.

submission rebuttal revision-1 camera-ready

Citations that exist

Language models make up citations that look right: a real author with a title they never wrote, a DOI that belongs to another paper, a link that was never there. meertex looks up every entry of your .bib files at doi.org, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, DBLP, arXiv and Open Library, and opens every link. It shows what does not match, with the publisher's value one click away, and it finds retracted papers and preprints that have been published since.

On request, an LLM reads each cited abstract and says whether it backs the sentence that cites it. When you checked an entry yourself, mark it. The mark lives in the entry and breaks as soon as anyone changes it.

DOI of another paper In no index Retracted Not the record's authors Dead link Checked by you 
$ meertex refs 2027-icse-green-llm check

Reviews you can follow up on

A reviewer comments on a passage of text, or double-clicks the PDF and meertex finds the line. A suggestion carries new text that the authors apply with one click. When a comment is resolved, meertex shows how the text changed, and the reviewer accepts it or reopens the comment. Everyone involved gets an email.

Folders, names and templates

Each coauthor sorts papers into their own folders. Names follow your pattern. Start from a publisher's template zip, such as ACM's acmart or IEEE's kit, and resubmit a rejected paper to the next venue as a copy that keeps its files.

{year}-{conference|lower}-{paper|lower} 2027-icse-green-llm

Ready when you are

Sign in at meertex.com with an emailed link or code, with no password to remember. Coauthors only need a browser. meertex is open source under the AGPL-3.0-or-later, so you can also run it on your own server.

Get started

Start writing on meertex.com

All you need is an email address. Your coauthors only need a browser.

1Sign in

Enter your email address and meertex mails you a link and a code. Either one signs you in, and your account is ready. Start your first paper in the browser right away, empty or from a publisher's template.

Sign in to meertex

2Invite your coauthors

In a paper's settings, invite people by email as editors, reviewers or viewers. They get an email, sign in the same way and write in the browser. There is nothing to install.

3Get the folder on your computer

To write in your own editor or with an agent, install the meertex command line tool from PyPI. It needs Python 3.11 or newer. meertex login mails you a code, and meertex checkout puts the paper in ~/papers. 

$ pipx install meertex
$ meertex login --server meertex.com
$ meertex checkout 2027-icse-green-llm

4Keep it in sync

meertex sync keeps every paper folder up to date while it runs. On Linux, meertex service install runs it in the background. On macOS and Windows, leave it running in a terminal window. 

$ meertex service install  # Linux
$ meertex sync             # macOS, Windows

Run your own server

meertex is open source, so you can also run it yourself, on a laptop with Docker or on a server with Coolify. The README explains how. The command line tool then signs in with meertex login --server and your server's address.

Command line

Full CLI support

Everything you do in the browser also works in a terminal, from the first checkout to the last review comment.

The command line tool

Papers, dates, questions, versions, changes and compiling, each paper addressed by its name. Every command can answer in JSON for agents.

$ meertex projects --outcome accepted
$ meertex show 2027-icse-green-llm
$ meertex diff 2027-icse-green-llm submission --word
$ meertex compile 2027-icse-green-llm

Review comments

Read the open comments together with the text they are about, answer them, apply suggestions and resolve, without leaving the terminal. 

$ meertex threads 2027-icse-green-llm
$ meertex reply 2027-icse-green-llm a1b2c3d4 "Done, see line 4."
$ meertex apply 2027-icse-green-llm 9f8e7d6c
$ meertex resolve 2027-icse-green-llm a1b2c3d4

Install and update

The command line tool is a Python package on PyPI. Every change to it is published as a new version, and pipx upgrade fetches it. 

$ pipx install meertex   # or: uv tool install meertex
$ pipx upgrade meertex
$ meertex --help

For agents

Let an LLM start your papers

Install the meertex skill for your LLM agent. It uses the command line tool, so install that first.

From a call for papers to a new paper

Paste a conference URL into your LLM agent, and the meertex-new-paper skill does the rest.

  1. 1It reads the call for papers and follows the links to the dates, the submission rules and the template.
  2. 2It takes the venue's fields and every deadline with its time zone, and leaves out what the page does not say.
  3. 3It creates the paper with the publisher's template and puts the folder in ~/papers/<name>.
$ mkdir -p $SKILLS/meertex-new-paper
$ curl -o $SKILLS/meertex-new-paper/SKILL.md \
    https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ribalba/meertex/main/skills/meertex-new-paper/SKILL.md
View SKILL.md on GitHub

$SKILLS is the folder your agent loads its skills from. In a clone of the repository, make install-skill links it instead.
